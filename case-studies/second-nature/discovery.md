# Second Nature — discovery v0.1
Status: concept; no participant research conducted. Not a clinical, financial advisory or productivity scoring tool.

## Problem hypothesis
Adults juggling work, personal commitments and aspirations may struggle to choose among worthwhile activities when time, money and energy are limited. Investigate whether explicit trade-offs and reversible decisions help more than task accumulation.

## Initial audience hypothesis
Working adults with competing discretionary priorities. Exclude urgent medical, safety and essential financial decisions from recommendation logic. No diagnosis or demographic inference.

## Research plan
Recruit 6–8 consenting adults with varied schedules; interview about a recent real decision, alternatives, constraints, consequences and current workarounds. Ask for counterexamples. Optional three-day diary of decisions, with no sensitive amounts or health records required. Synthesize anonymized observations, distinguish quotes from interpretations, record dissent. No invented participants, quotes or metrics.

## Interview guide
1. Tell me about the last time two worthwhile activities competed for your time.
2. What alternatives did you consider? What was non-negotiable?
3. What information did you lack? How did you decide?
4. What happened afterward? Would you choose differently?
5. Show your current workaround if comfortable.
6. When would a decision companion be intrusive or unhelpful?

## Product hypothesis and scope
Mobile-first, local-first decision companion. Capture a decision, alternatives, available time, optional spending ceiling and self-described energy. Separate hard constraints from preferences. Compare feasible options transparently; never claim an optimal life choice. Include defer, do nothing, and revise. Record an optional reflection without streaks or guilt. No account, external integrations, notifications or AI inference in MVP.

## Decision architecture
Inputs: available minutes, optional budget, energy preference, user-defined priority, alternatives with estimated time/cost/effort and intended benefit. Reject invalid negative or missing required time estimates. Mark unknown cost as unknown, not zero. Flag constraint conflicts; show reasons, never silently discard alternatives. User can override preferences, but hard constraints require explicit revision. No numeric wellness or productivity score. Persist locally only with clear delete control.

## Synthetic acceptance scenarios
A: 45 minutes, two options of 30 and 60 minutes; show second exceeds time by 15 minutes.
B: low energy, two feasible options; show effort differences without moral judgment.
C: unknown cost and explicit spending ceiling; mark affordability unverified.
D: no feasible option; offer defer or revise constraints.
E: user changes mind; preserve agency and allow undo.
F: keyboard, reduced motion, 390px mobile and 1440px desktop; verify labels, focus, overflow, feedback and persistence.

## Validation plan
Compare unaided choice with prototype-supported choice using counterbalanced synthetic scenarios. Observe comprehension of constraints, ability to revise and explain choice, perceived pressure and usability. Proposed success criteria: at least 5 of 6 participants can identify the trade-off and revise a choice without assistance; no critical accessibility defects. These are targets, not results.

## Portfolio evidence
Publish only actual research and verified test results. Clearly label synthetic examples. Do not deploy or change public portfolio until prototype and review gates pass.
